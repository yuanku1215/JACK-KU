Abstract

Levee systems protect riverfront areas from flooding but often create barriers that limit local mobility. In many communities, residents face restricted access to riverside parks and essential services due to a lack of crossing facilities. This study introduces an integrated framework that combines spatial analysis with multi-objective optimization to guide the siting and improvement of levee crossings. The framework evaluates five objectives: construction cost, sidewalk improvement cost, population coverage, spatial equity, and crash risk, using road networks, population data, and crash records. Two optimization methods are applied and compared: the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm III (NSGA III) and Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA). NSGA III tends to concentrate solutions within a specific region of the Pareto front, while SQA performs better in less-explored areas. SQA uses weight-driven scalarization to target specific planning priorities, while NSGA III supports broader exploration across objectives. Together, they form a complementary toolkit: NSGA III identifies promising trade-offs, and SQA refines solutions based on policy preferences. A simulation of 40 candidate sites reveals how cost, accessibility, equity, and safety interact, highlighting underserved and high-risk zones. This dual-method approach supports informed, equitable, and implementable infrastructure decisions in flood-prone urban environments.

Primary Optimization Objectives

The facility location problem is modeled across five conflicting dimensions to identify the Pareto-optimal frontier for urban decision-making.

Construction Cost

Minimizing total capital expenditure for new structural facilities.

Sidewalk Retrofitting

Accounting for auxiliary costs required for seamless connectivity.

Demand Coverage

Maximizing weighted population served within walkable catchments.

Spatial Equity

Minimizing disparity to ensure fair infrastructure distribution.

Crash Risk Exposure

Reducing hazards by penalizing sites with high crash densities.

Decision Support Platform Visualization

A Multi-dimensional GIS platform developed to synchronize LOD1 building geometry with thematic spatial indicators for people-centered planning.

Impact on Industrial Engineering & Operations Research

This research bridges the gap between spatial data science and formal mathematical programming, offering new insights into urban systems engineering.

  • Combinatorial Optimization: Formulates levee-crossing siting as a Binary Integer Programming problem, advancing Facility Location Theory.
  • Hybrid Metaheuristics: Benchmarks NSGA-III against Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA), evaluating solver performance in non-convex discrete search spaces.
  • Decision Science: Employs Pareto Front Analysis and weight-driven scalarization to navigate trade-offs between socio-economic objectives.
  • Algorithmic Efficiency: Explores the practical application of QUBO (Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization) for large-scale urban infrastructure modeling.
