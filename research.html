Research Overview

Levee systems protect riverfront areas from flooding but often create barriers that limit local mobility. In many urban communities, residents face restricted access to riverside parks due to a lack of crossing facilities. This research introduces an integrated framework that combines Spatial Analysis with Multi-objective Optimization to guide the siting and improvement of levee crossings.

Using Sanchong and Luzhou districts in New Taipei City as a case study, we utilized GIS road network analysis and Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to quantify accessibility. The model evaluates trade-offs using NSGA-III and explores large-scale combinatorial search spaces via Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA).

Evaluation Framework

1. Population Coverage

Maximizing the number of residents served within defined network-based distance thresholds.

2. Travel Efficiency

Minimizing average walking time and detours for residents to reach riverfront spaces.

3. Facility Cost

Balancing the scale of construction (stairs vs. ramps) with available municipal budgets.

4. Spatial Equity

Ensuring an even distribution of accessibility to prevent service gaps in underserved neighborhoods.

5. Safety & Risk

Considering traffic safety and environmental impacts near potential crossing corridors.

Methodological Pipeline

Spatial Preprocessing

Generating candidate crossing sites and computing service areas through GIS network analysis. Population density is mapped via KDE to identify high-demand clusters.

Optimization Engine

Comparing Evolutionary Algorithms (NSGA-II/III) with Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA) using QUBO encoding to find the most efficient Pareto-front solutions.
