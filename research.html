Abstract

Levee systems protect riverfront areas from flooding but often create barriers that limit local mobility. In many communities, residents face restricted access to riverside parks and essential services due to a lack of crossing facilities. This study introduces an integrated framework that combines spatial analysis with multi-objective optimization to guide the siting and improvement of levee crossings. The framework evaluates five objectives: construction cost, sidewalk improvement cost, population coverage, spatial equity, and crash risk, using road networks, population data, and crash records. Two optimization methods are applied and compared: the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm III (NSGA III) and Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA). NSGA III tends to concentrate solutions within a specific region of the Pareto front, while SQA performs better in less-explored areas. SQA uses weight-driven scalarization to target specific planning priorities, while NSGA III supports broader exploration across objectives. Together, they form a complementary toolkit: NSGA III identifies promising trade-offs, and SQA refines solutions based on policy preferences. A simulation of 40 candidate sites reveals how cost, accessibility, equity, and safety interact, highlighting underserved and high-risk zones. This dual-method approach supports informed, equitable, and implementable infrastructure decisions in flood-prone urban environments.

Primary Optimization Objectives

Our research evaluates the facility location problem across five conflicting dimensions to ensure comprehensive decision support.

Construction Cost

Minimizing total budget requirements for new facility establishment and structural updates.

Sidewalk Improvement

Accounting for the necessary retrofit costs to ensure seamless pedestrian access to the levee.

Population Coverage

Maximizing the number of residents served within the 500m walkable network threshold.

Spatial Equity

Reducing accessibility disparity between neighborhoods to ensure social justice in infrastructure.

Crash Risk (Safety)

Minimizing exposure to traffic hazards by analyzing historical crash records and intersection safety.

Decision Support Platform

A Multi-dimensional GIS platform was built to visualize the 3D campus/urban environment and evaluate the impacts of different facility configurations.

Impact on IEOR

This research bridges the gap between spatial data science and operations research, providing novel insights into several IEOR domains.

  • Combinatorial Optimization: It models complex siting decisions as a Binary Programming problem, contributing to Facility Location Theory under multi-objective constraints.
  • Metaheuristics Benchmarking: By comparing NSGA-III (Evolutionary) and SQA (Quantum-inspired), it evaluates the convergence and diversity of solvers in large-scale discrete search spaces.
  • Decision Science: The use of Pareto fronts and weight-driven scalarization provides a systematic way for decision-makers to handle Conflicting Objectives in urban systems.
  • Algorithmic Efficiency: The application of QUBO (Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization) encoding explores the potential of Quantum Computing for real-world infrastructure problems.
