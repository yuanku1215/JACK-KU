Featured Publication

Conference Paper | WCTR 2026 Toulouse

Integrating Multi-objective Optimization and Spatial Analysis for Levee Crossing Facilities

Abstract: Levee systems often act as mobility barriers in riverfront communities. This research addresses the siting problem by evaluating five objectives: population coverage, travel time, construction cost, spatial equity, and environmental safety risk. Using Sanchong and Luzhou as study areas, we benchmarked NSGA-III against Simulated Quantum Annealing (SQA) to find optimal trade-offs.

Optimization Objectives

Our research framework considers five conflicting goals to ensure a people-centered planning outcome.

Population Coverage

Maximizing the total demand points served within walking network distance thresholds (e.g., 500m).

Travel Efficiency

Minimizing detour ratios and total travel time for residents accessing riverfront parks.

Implementation Cost

Balancing infrastructure costs based on facility types such as stairs, ramps, or elevators.

Spatial Equity

Using accessibility indices to identify underserved clusters and ensure uniform service distribution.

Risk Mitigation

Incorporating traffic safety data and Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to avoid high-risk crossing zones.

Technical Methodology

Spatial Data Engineering

Integration of road network data (OSM/GIS), population grids, and levee geometry using Python and ArcGIS Pro to generate candidate sites.

Evolutionary & Quantum Algorithms

Comparative analysis of NSGA-III and SQA with QUBO encoding to navigate large-scale combinatorial search spaces for Pareto solutions.
