Overview

The goal of this workshop was to give participants a practical entry point into quantum algorithms for optimization. Instead of starting from physics heavy concepts, the content is organized around an optimization narrative: energy landscapes, local minima, and search strategies.

The workshop progresses from classical annealing ideas to simulated annealing, then to quantum annealing, highlighting what changes and what stays the same when reformulating problems as QUBO. I also addressed common implementation issues such as constraint encoding, penalty scaling, and debugging energy functions, and concluded with hands on labs.

Teaching design choice: build intuition first from an optimization viewpoint, then introduce quantum concepts as extensions, so participants can connect the ideas to their own research problems immediately.

Audience and Learning Goals

Target Audience

Geomatics MS and PhD students and cross department learners who want an applied view of quantum optimization.

Learning Goal

Understand how optimization problems can be reformulated into QUBO and solved via annealing based methods.

Outcome

Participants can interpret QUBO energy terms, implement a basic solver pipeline, and debug formulations.

Workshop Structure

The slide deck follows a learning path designed to reduce intimidation and maximize transfer to real optimization tasks.

Part 1: Optimization Intuition

Energy landscapes, local minima, exploration versus exploitation, and why annealing works as a search principle.

Part 2: Simulated Annealing

Temperature schedules, acceptance probability, neighborhood design, and practical pitfalls when implementing SA.

Part 3: Quantum Annealing

Quantum fluctuations and tunneling intuition, and mapping optimization problems to Ising and QUBO models.

Part 4: QUBO Modeling Practice

Constructing QUBO, encoding constraints, penalty selection, and validating the energy function end to end.

Common implementation issues explicitly covered
  • Penalty tuning: too small leads to infeasible solutions, too large dominates numerically and harms exploration.
  • Constraint encoding: equality constraints require careful quadratic expansion and scaling.
  • Energy debugging: verify better solutions reduce energy, test with known feasible cases.
  • Variable indexing: incorrect mapping between tuples and bit indices silently breaks models.
  • Interpretability: each QUBO term must be explained in the original problem context.

QUBO: From Basic Math to an Executable Model

A key part of the workshop is making QUBO feel constructible. Participants learn to start from binary variables, write objectives, then add constraints as quadratic penalties to form an executable energy model.

Core message: QUBO is a disciplined way to express a problem as min xᵀQx with binary decisions, where every term has a clear meaning.
Goal: minimize xᵀ Q x , where x ∈ {0,1}ⁿ

1) Write objective in binary variables
2) Convert constraints into quadratic penalties
3) Combine: E(x) = objective(x) + λ · penalty(x)
4) Validate: feasible solutions have low penalty, improvements reduce E(x)
